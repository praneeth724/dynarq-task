Answer each question **only from the files in `sources/`**. After each answer, cite
where it comes from, for example `Source 1, Article 3(1)`. If the sources do not

1. How does the directive define statutory interest for late payment?

   Answer: It is **simple interest** for late payment at a rate equal to the
   **reference rate plus at least eight percentage points** (Source 1, Article 2(6)).
   For a euro country the reference rate is either the rate the ECB applied to its most
   recent main refinancing operations, or the marginal rate from variable-rate tenders
   for those operations; for a non-euro country it is the equivalent rate of its
   national central bank (Source 1, Article 2(7)(a) and (b)). The rate is fixed per half
   year: the rate in force on 1 January applies to the first half, the rate on 1 July to
   the second (Source 1, Article 3(2)). "Interest for late payment" can also be a rate
   the businesses agreed, subject to Article 7 (Source 1, Article 2(5)).

2. The owner's note says the EU maximum late fee is 25 EUR. Is that right?

   Answer: **No, the sources do not support it.** The 25 EUR figure appears only in the
   owner's own note, which is unchecked belief (Source 2, point 1). The directive sets
   no maximum: when interest for late payment becomes payable, the creditor is entitled
   to "as a minimum, a fixed sum of EUR 40" as compensation for its own recovery costs
   (Source 1, Article 6(1)), plus reasonable compensation for recovery costs above that
   sum, such as a lawyer or debt collection agency (Source 1, Article 6(3)). So EUR 40 is
   a floor, not a ceiling, and 25 EUR is below it. A term that excludes this
   compensation is presumed grossly unfair (Source 1, Article 7(3)). How the owner's
   country has implemented this is not in the sources.

3. Does the business have to send a reminder before it is entitled to interest and to
   the fixed compensation sum?

   Answer: **No.** Between businesses the creditor is entitled to interest for late
   payment "without the necessity of a reminder", provided it has fulfilled its own
   obligations and has not received the amount due on time, unless the debtor is not
   responsible for the delay (Source 1, Article 3(1)(a) and (b)); the same applies when
   the debtor is a public authority (Source 1, Article 4(1)). Interest runs from the day
   after the payment date in the contract (Source 1, Article 3(3)(a)). The fixed sum is
   also payable "without the necessity of a reminder" (Source 1, Article 6(2)). So the
   owner's belief in Source 2, point 2, is not supported by the directive.

4. What is the current ECB reference rate?

   Answer: **Not in the sources.** The directive only defines what the reference rate is
   and which date's rate applies (Source 1, Article 2(7) and Article 3(2)); it gives no
   figure, and the owner's note gives none either (Source 2, point 4). To answer I would
   need the ECB's official published rate for its main refinancing operations in force on
   1 July 2026 (the rate for the second half of 2026, Article 3(2)(b)), and to know which
   country's law applies, because a non-euro country uses its own central bank's rate
   (Article 2(7)(b)); one sample invoice is in USD. Until it is checked, it should not go
   into the final notice.

## Extra note for the owner (point 3 of the owner's note)

A 90-day payment term between businesses: the directive says the contractual payment
period shall not exceed 60 calendar days, **unless otherwise expressly agreed in the
contract and provided it is not grossly unfair to the creditor** (Source 1, Article
3(5); Article 7 for "grossly unfair"). So 90 days is not automatically forbidden, but it
needs an express agreement and must not be grossly unfair. National law is not in the
sources.

## What this means for the reminder messages

The messages make no claims about fees, interest or the ECB rate. The 25 EUR fee is not
supported (question 2), the ECB rate is unknown (question 4), and adding either is the
owner's decision after checking national law.
